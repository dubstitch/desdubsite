01 / CMS architecture, custom components, experience ownership

175 Years. One Connected Experience.

A major anniversary deserves more than a collection of links. I built and expanded an interconnected digital toolkit in Higher Logic Collaborate, with supporting pages and resources designed to help people find what they need and take action.

Higher Logic CollaborateCustom code and CSSComponent repairsInformation architecturePublishing and permissions
THE EXPERIENCE AT A GLANCE175th Anniversary Toolkit

A connected resource experience spanning the Celebration Kit, grants content, custom page components and publishing fixes.

The assignment

Deliver a multi-page anniversary resource experience under urgent deadlines. The work grew as new campaign needs appeared, including the Celebration Kit, a Grants application experience, and student-focused content, alongside the broader anniversary resources and Built to Shine.

What I actually built

I wrote front-end code and CSS for many of the presentation components used across the experience. Where existing Collaborate components were broken or unsuitable, I diagnosed the problems, repaired them, and adapted the output to the intended layout. This was hands-on implementation as well as content publishing.

The structure behind it

I organized pages, resource paths, navigation, and page relationships as one content system rather than unrelated campaign posts. I also worked through breadcrumb structure, access permissions, public page visibility and application form paths, so links could take visitors where they were meant to go.

Why it matters

The experience demonstrates ownership of both the user journey and the CMS details behind it. It required implementing, troubleshooting, structuring and publishing under rapidly changing requirements.

02 / Platform migration, reusable experiences

Make the CMS Work for the Content.

When an enterprise platform cannot deliver the experience a project needs out of the box, someone has to bridge the gap between what the content requires and what the CMS can actually do.

CMS migrationTen Contacts pagesSponsor grid and sliderReusable patternsResponsive troubleshooting
THE EXPERIENCE AT A GLANCECollaborate Migration

Ten structured Contacts pages, reusable leadership content and a sponsor presentation built for Higher Logic Collaborate.

The assignment

Support ASCE content and community experiences during work in Higher Logic Collaborate, including ten Contacts pages and more structured ways of presenting leadership and sponsor information.

What I implemented

I built the section Contacts pages, worked on leadership presentation patterns, and developed or adapted sponsor grids and slider components. I used front-end troubleshooting and CMS configuration to solve layout, component and responsive presentation problems.

What made it difficult

Existing components did not reliably support every content need. I had to understand the system, isolate its limitations and find reusable solutions that maintained a consistent experience across different pages.

Why it matters

This work shows CMS fluency beyond routine publishing, including component-level problem solving, scalable content patterns and collaboration with stakeholders under practical constraints.

03 / Event website implementation, accessibility, UX

A Complex Event, A Clearer Experience.

Event experiences bring together a lot of moving pieces. I supported the published ASCE 2027 site through hands-on implementation, content presentation, accessibility improvements and interaction troubleshooting.

SitecoreResponsive implementationKeyboard focusEvent navigationComponent QA
THE EXPERIENCE AT A GLANCEASCE 2027

Event page architecture, navigation, custom presentation components and responsive updates in Sitecore.

What I worked on

I implemented and refined major event-site elements, including the header, hero, schedule-related components and interactive presentation. I also addressed mobile and responsive display needs.

Accessibility and interaction

I improved visible keyboard focus and active states in interactive elements, including accordion and dropdown behavior, so interaction was not communicated through hover alone.

How I approached the experience

I considered how visitors move between program information, navigation and event details, while balancing design requirements, CMS behavior and publishing realities.

What is still evolving

The broader ASCE 2027 UX redesign has been under discussion. This case study describes the implementation and fixes I have already completed, not a redesign that has not yet shipped.
