01 / Front-end engineering, information architecture, interactive design

Two Programs. One Connected Experience.

WCFCC was a multi-page website for a youth football and cheer camp. I developed a bold, cohesive site experience to help families understand the programs, training schedules, coaching information and registration process. After a successful 2025 season, the planned 2026 camp was postponed until further notice. The hope is to return in 2027. I preserved the complete planned website as a navigable portfolio archive.

HTML, CSS & JavaScriptMulti-page architectureResponsive layoutsInteractive componentsPortfolio archive
ARCHIVED WEBSITE DEMOWCFCC Football & Cheer

Distinct program pages, schedule information, accordions, carousels and a consistent visual system designed around the family journey.

The challenge

Organize a detailed camp experience around the questions parents actually need answered. The website needed to explain two different programs without fragmenting the navigation or losing the shared WCFCC identity.

What I built

I built the homepage and dedicated football, cheer, coaches, schedules, FAQ, contact and registration pages, alongside supporting program resources. I created the site styles and interactive JavaScript to support program comparisons, tabs, carousels and expandable content.

Design and implementation

The visual system uses high-contrast typography, bright accents, differentiated cheer and football treatments, responsive layouts and reusable interaction patterns. I structured the pages so people could explore program details, then find practical information through consistent navigation and calls to action.

Why it is an archive

The planned 2026 season was postponed following the successful 2025 camp, so live registration, document forms, payment pathways and contact actions are intentionally disabled in this archive. Those controls remain visible with lock indicators, while internal page navigation and on-page interactions demonstrate the original design.

02 / CMS architecture, custom components, experience ownership

175 Years. One Connected Experience.

A major anniversary deserves more than a collection of links. I built and expanded an interconnected digital toolkit in Higher Logic Collaborate, with supporting pages and resources designed to help people find what they need and take action.

Higher Logic CollaborateCustom code and CSSComponent repairsInformation architecturePublishing and permissions
THE EXPERIENCE AT A GLANCE175th Anniversary Toolkit

A connected resource experience spanning the Celebration Kit, grants content, custom page components and publishing fixes.

The assignment

Deliver a multi-page anniversary resource experience under urgent deadlines. The work grew as new campaign needs appeared, including the Celebration Kit, a Grants application experience, and student-focused content, alongside the broader anniversary resources and Built to Shine.

What I actually built

I built custom page components, including a slider and modal experience, and developed tailored layouts for anniversary resources. When Higher Logic Collaborate's component library could not support the intended design or an existing component was broken, I wrote and injected code and CSS to extend or repair it. This went well beyond configuring existing content blocks.

The structure behind it

I organized pages, resource paths, navigation, and page relationships as one content system rather than unrelated campaign posts. I also worked through breadcrumb structure, access permissions, public page visibility and application form paths, so links could take visitors where they were meant to go.

Technical implementation

I troubleshot image sizing, navigation paths, breadcrumb behavior, publishing permissions, public page visibility and grants application destinations. I checked the published experience as well as the authoring setup and documented the toolkit with full page screenshots, direct page links and detailed implementation notes. The challenge was delivering a connected, maintainable experience under changing requirements while keeping the work easy to review.

Why it matters

The toolkit demonstrates how I combine content strategy, custom development, quality assurance and documentation. I owned the user journey as well as the CMS troubleshooting needed to make the experience work, and created a traceable record of the implementation.

Make the CMS Work for the Content.

When an enterprise platform cannot deliver the experience a project needs out of the box, someone has to bridge the gap between what the content requires and what the CMS can actually do.

THE EXPERIENCE AT A GLANCECollaborate Migration

Ten structured Contacts pages, reusable leadership content and a sponsor presentation built for Higher Logic Collaborate.

The assignment

Support ASCE content and community experiences during work in Higher Logic Collaborate, including ten Contacts pages and more structured ways of presenting leadership and sponsor information.

What I implemented

I built the section Contacts pages, worked on leadership presentation patterns, and developed or adapted sponsor grids and slider components. I used front-end troubleshooting and CMS configuration to solve layout, component and responsive presentation problems.

What made it difficult

Existing components did not reliably support every content need. I had to understand the system, isolate its limitations and find reusable solutions that maintained a consistent experience across different pages.

Technical implementation

I configured and customized Higher Logic Collaborate pages and presentation components, including ten Contacts pages, leadership layouts and sponsor grids and sliders. I troubleshot responsive behavior and worked around component limitations while preserving repeatable patterns for publishers. The emphasis was on making solutions reusable rather than solving the same display problem on every page.

Why it matters

This work shows CMS fluency beyond routine publishing, including component-level problem solving, scalable content patterns and collaboration with stakeholders under practical constraints.

05 / Personal project, interface engineering, data-driven UI

A More Personal Way to Keep a List.

Wade List is a developing family wishlist built for a personal shopper based in Thailand. Instead of exchanging lists and screenshots, the family can reference exact products, photographs, source listings, intended recipients and notes in one editorial browsing experience. Its GitHub repository is named Dublist.

In progressVanilla JavaScript modulesJSON-driven contentScroll-driven UIProduct modals & carousels
PERSONAL PROJECT / WORK IN PROGRESSWade List · Personal Shopper

Category transitions, product cards and detail modals for an experimental, visual-first shopping wishlist.

The concept

Give a personal shopper a reliable source for products wanted by Des, Vada and Keith. The experience is organized by women's and men's product categories, with details and recipient tags that help the shopper find the requested item.

What exists

The prototype includes two connected shopping wishlists, full-screen category crossfades, accessible product carousels, read-only product detail modals and three published activity indicators for new, updated and removed requests. Separate JSON catalogs keep product details independent from layout code.

Engineering approach

I organized the interface in reusable CSS and JavaScript modules, using data-driven rendering and interaction logic for category views and modals. The prototype also includes a mobile-oriented browsing treatment rather than simply shrinking its desktop layout.

Still in progress

Some products still use placeholder art, example listings and historical sample prices. Item matching can be specified as exact or similar, but a secure shopper login and persistent shopper notes are still planned. Published activity reflects explicit catalog updates, not real-time notifications.
